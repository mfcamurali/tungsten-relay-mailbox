# EDGE — what we actually know, as of 2026-08-30

Written at Manuel's request, consolidating one day's conversation and measurement. Every number
here comes from a script in `research_layer/` with a known-answer self-test, run against the real
50,000-bar export. Where something is not decidable from the data we hold, it says so.

---

## 1. The word "edge" has been doing three jobs, and they are not the same job

| | What it claims | How we measure it | What we found |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Directional edge** | a high score means price goes that way | information coefficient | absent, at every horizon, after correction |
| **Selective edge** | in *some* moments the score is right, and we can tell which in advance | variance of local slopes | not present in the blend; **untested per component** |
| **Structural edge** | direction is a coin flip but the payoff geometry is not | expectancy per trade | current geometry is **negative**, and the data cannot yet say what the best one is |

Almost every argument we have had was two of these being compared as if they were one.

---

## 2. The arithmetic, stated once

**Per trade:**

```
E  =  p·W  −  (1−p)·L
```

Three levers, not four. "Winning more often" and "losing less frequently" are the *same number* —
p and (1−p) are complements. Any claim that lists them separately has double-counted.

**Why a pooled IC cannot see a flipping edge.** If the true relationship is `r = β(z)·s + ε` with
β depending on a latent local state z, then the pooled IC is proportional to **E[β]** — the *mean*
local slope. If β flips sign across states, E[β] → 0 no matter how large |β| is anywhere. The edge,
in that world, lives in **Var(β)**, and a pooled IC is structurally blind to it.

**The coupling that makes it hard.** p, W and L cannot be chosen independently. Widen the target →
W rises, p falls. Tighten the stop → L falls, p falls faster. A bracket choice slides you along one
curve; it does not let you pick three numbers.

## 3. What was measured, and what each measurement does *not* say

### 3.1 Directional edge — absent, and now properly corrected

- IC published as a **curve across ten horizons**, not a point (C3.3).
- Multiplicity deflated against a **ledger of every test the program has run** — 182 registered,
  deflated critical z **3.638** against the undeflated 1.960 (C3.4).
- Every interval computed on the **uniqueness-weighted effective sample**, not the raw bar count —
  overlapping labels mean 50,000 rows are not 50,000 independent facts (C3.6).

**Result: no horizon survives.** l=1 and l=2 are significant undeflated and lose to the deflated
bar. The only survivors are two *contemporaneous same-bar* log-return ICs, which are explicitly not
forward-predictive tests.

> This is the strongest form of the null this project has ever measured. It is **not** the
> edge/no-edge verdict — that is criterion C4, and it stops and asks.

### 3.2 Selective edge — the blend says no, the components are untested

Under a true null, the Fisher-z of a local IC over n independent observations is N(0, 1/(n−3)), so
standardising each local z by its own √(n−3) gives a collection with **variance exactly 1**. Excess
variance is local structure; a mean staying at zero is the sign flipping.

```
h=1 (no overlap — the clean read)
  time windows   ratio 0.86   control 0.77   p 0.84
  state cells    ratio 1.34   control 0.92   p 0.058
```

No excess. **But this measures the blend, and that is a hole big enough for the whole thesis:** if
one component leans one way in a cell and another leans the other, the blend is flat there *and*
flat pooled. The cancellation happens *before the score exists*, so a test on the score is looking
downstream of the destruction.

`component_edge_test.py` tests exactly that, and proves it can: with a sign-flipping edge planted
in one component and another built to cancel it, it reports **blend 0.75 (invisible), planted
component 70.4 (visible), control 0.95, cancellation index 28.8**.

**It needs one calibration run.** The 21-column export carrying the per-component values is already
written into the EA and already switched on; the file on disk simply predates it.

### 3.3 Structural edge — the stop is inside the noise

```
median adverse excursion   1.55 ATR
stop in use                1.50 ATR
stop breached              55.9% of bars
target (2.5 ATR) reached   30.1% of bars

current bracket (2.5/1.5): E = −0.0503R, p = 0.391, break-even p = 0.406 — rank 63 of 80
```

**The stop is hit almost twice as often as the target, regardless of direction.** That comparison
needs no ordering assumption and no window beyond the recorded extremes — it is the most robust
finding in this document.

It is also the precise refutation of "lose as little as possible" as a *strategy*: the stop is
already tight enough to be triggered by noise more often than the target is reached by signal.
Tightening it lowers L and lowers p faster.

## 4. Four artefacts found and killed in our own analysis

Recorded because the day's lesson is that the instruments were wrong more often than the system.

1. **Overlap inflation.** The local-edge test first reported ratios of 3.26 at h=5 and 7.27 at
   h=21 — a spectacular confirmation of the thesis. Both are *exactly the overlap factor*: 3.26/5
   and 7.27/21 are below 1. The permutation control could not catch it and never could, because
   shuffling the score destroys the serial dependence the artefact requires.
2. **The absent stop.** The bracket sweep first ranked SL 3.0 top at +0.1625R — with an average
   loss of 1.05R, which is the arithmetic saying the stop was never hit. A 3.0 ATR stop is reached
   in **3.4%** of bars. A no-stop strategy always wins over a bounded window, because the window
   deletes the tail it would have paid for.
3. **First-touch from the wrong timestamp.** `bars_to_mfe`/`bars_to_mae` are times to the
   *extreme*, not to a threshold crossing. Using them for ordering is systematically wrong, worst
   exactly where both thresholds sit inside their extremes. **The sweep therefore cannot decide
   which bracket is best**, and now says so instead of ranking anyway.
4. **The un-failable check.** C3.1's net-b test was a regex that reported 119/119 passing; a bare
   `R` matches almost any word boundary. Rewritten to require a payoff figure actually formatted
   into the line, the honest answer is **24 of 118**.

## 6. The structural finding: calibration optimises the wrong quantity

Phase 3B's weight search maximises

```
comp = trainWR × min(PF, 3.0) × log₁₀(1 + n) × (1 + evBonus)
evBonus = min(0.30, (avgWin − avgLoss) × 5)
```

Expectancy enters as a **bonus capped at 30%** on a win-rate × profit-factor product. The search is
dominated by **p** — the one lever every measurement says will not move.

Worse, the coupled quantities are optimised in **separate phases**: weights in 3B, brackets in
3C/3D. The system picks p first, then picks a bracket for it. (p, W, L) are never chosen jointly,
though they lie on one curve.

And the arm gate is a **pooled** win rate (0.58 / 0.62). If the edge is selective, that averages the
tradeable subset in with everything else — the exact operation that destroys it. A system required
to clear a global win rate cannot express *"I trade 2% of bars and I'm right on those."*

**The right objective already exists in the code.** P6's quarantine drops a regime on **E < 0.10R**
— per regime, expectancy-based, exactly the right form. It is applied at the end to *reject*,
rather than at the front to *select*.

## 7. What is decidable, and with what

| Question | Decidable? |
|---|---|
| Does the blended score predict direction? | **Answered: no**, at every horizon, after correction |
| Is the blend hiding component-level local edge? | **One calibration run.** Instrument built and proven |
| Which exit geometry maximises expectancy? | **Not from recorded extremes.** Needs the price path — Phase 3B already replays against it |
| Does the system learn? | **Three calibrations.** The trend record now exists and fills itself |
| Does it behave as predicted forward? | **One arming + the declared wait.** Pre-registration now written at arm time |

## 8. What to build

In dependency order. All terminal-free to write.

1. **Change the search objective to expectancy**, and optimise weights *jointly* with the bracket
   instead of sequentially. This is "train hard at getting the equation as high as we can",
   implemented. Biggest lever.
2. **Gate on expectancy per regime instead of pooled win rate** — the quarantine's own criterion,
   moved from the end to the front.
3. **Apply meta-labelling** to the export: primary = the existing score, secondary = *should we act
   here*, trained on regime, trend strength and volatility context. This is where "a strong trend in
   a trending regime is a good sign" stops being an intuition and becomes a measured weight.
4. **Have Phase 3B sweep and report the expectancy curve** against the real path — the ordering
   problem in §4.3 does not exist there.
5. **Route the live scorer through the six canonical helpers** (behind an identity flag, default
   off, proven equal by the existing parity test before it is ever switched on). This is C1.3, and
   it is a build, not a run.
6. **Wire the cell layer's decision path** — `LE_IsTradeable`, the cell-informed brackets, the
   driver fingerprint — so it can be measured rather than inferred.

---

## 9. The honest summary

We have not found a directional edge, and today's work makes that null as solid as it can honestly
be made. That is not the same as having tested the thesis Manuel actually holds — that edge is
local, moment-to-moment, sign-flipping, and that timing rather than direction is the make-or-break.
Those are measured downstream of a blend that may be destroying the very thing they describe, by a
calibration that optimises the one quantity we cannot move, gated by a pooled rate that averages
away selectivity by construction — with the machinery to do it properly built, correct, and wired
to nothing.

**The next real answer costs one calibration run. The one after that costs a change of objective.**
